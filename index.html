How I structured each post — the GEO/AEO formula
Step 01 — Query-matched title
Write the title as the exact question
Every post title mirrors the conversational query a user types into Google — or asks ChatGPT. This signals intent clearly to both traditional crawlers and AI models scanning for answer eligibility.
✓ "What happens if I place an order while the stock market is closed?"
✗ "Stock Market Order Handling Guide"
Step 02 — Direct-answer opening
Answer first, explain second
The answer comes in sentence 1 — completely and concisely — before any elaboration. This targets the AI Overview extraction pattern where Google pulls the most direct answer from the opening of the page body.
✓ "A stop order triggers a new trade when price reaches a level. A stop loss closes an existing position at a specified level."
— answer complete in 2 sentences
Step 03 — Layered structure
Answer → context → nuance
After the direct answer, a brief elaboration adds context for readers who need more. A final layer covers edge cases or caveats — e.g. slippage risk, gapping markets — without burying the core answer.
"Why did my stock trade open at a different price?"
→ Define slippage (answer)
→ When it happens (context)
→ How limit orders help (nuance)
Step 04 — PAA & AI Overview targeting
Match real search patterns
Each post targets a query that appears in People Also Ask boxes, Google autocomplete, or AI Overview answer surfaces. Topic clusters are grouped — e.g. all order-type questions — to build topical authority and enable internal linking.
Cluster example — Stock Orders:
"market order vs limit order" · "what is a stop loss" · "what is slippage" · "orders outside market hours"
Real examples — annotated
Example 1 — Definitional distinction
Q: What is the difference between a stop order and a stop loss order?
A: A stop order (stop-entry order) triggers a new trade when the price reaches a specified level — used to enter a position when a price condition is met. A stop loss is a risk-management order on an existing open position that is designed to close it when the price reaches a specified level. A stop loss does not guarantee that the position will close at the specified price, particularly during fast-moving or gapping markets.
✓ Answers in sentence 1 ✓ Distinguishes two related terms ✓ Adds caveat (gapping) for accuracy
Example 2 — Concept explanation with market context
Q: Why do stock prices sometimes jump when the market opens?
A: This is called a gap. News released while the market was closed — earnings results, economic data, geopolitical events — causes traders and institutions to reassess a stock's value. When trading resumes, the opening price reflects this new consensus, appearing as a visible gap up or down from the previous day's close. Positions held overnight are exposed to this gap risk.
✓ Names the concept first (gap) ✓ Explains mechanism clearly ✓ Risk implication in final sentence
Example 3 — Practical how-to with formula
Q: How do I calculate the right position size for a stock trade?
A: Position size can be calculated based on the amount you are prepared to risk and the distance between your entry price and stop-loss level. For example, if you choose to risk $100 and the difference between your entry price and stop-loss price is $2 per share, the position size would be 50 shares. For stock CFDs, also consider the contract specifications, leverage and applicable margin requirements.
✓ Formula + worked example ✓ Actionable in 3 sentences ✓ CFD-specific nuance added
